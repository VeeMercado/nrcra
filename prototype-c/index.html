Community knowledge, lived experience and shared learning.

Since early 2024, groups and organisations across the Northern Rivers have joined together to form the Resilience Alliance, a grassroots community-led network focused on collective care, preparedness and regional strength.

No community should have to build disaster resilience alone. The Resilience Alliance connects prepared communities so they can share knowledge and work together, not in parallel.

Our Focus

  1. 01 Growing the Resilience Alliance

    We are strengthening our network by clarifying who we are, why we come together and how we work. Through regular gatherings we are building a clearer picture of who is involved and how we connect.

  2. 02 Training and Resource Sharing

    We are making knowledge, practical tools and training easier to find through our Resilience Alliance Community Knowledge Hub. Our training is led by experienced locals, with the aim of strengthening our network from within the community.

  3. 03 Peer Support and Self Care

    Resilience work asks a lot of the people who do it, so we are committed to balanced wellbeing and collective care across our region. We are mapping the support already available, identifying good practice, with the aim to develop an adaptable wellbeing framework that groups can use to look after themselves and each other.

  4. 04 Funding and Collaborative Projects

    We are working towards a more sustainable and collaborative approach to funding for resilience work across the region. Identifying shared opportunities we aim to reduce competition and increase collective impact.

  5. 05 Advocacy

    We believe our collective voice is stronger than any single group’s. By understanding our own network, identifying key stakeholders and building advocacy skills among groups and organisations, we are developing a collaborative approach to advocating for resilient communities across the region.

About us

We know communities are stronger when they’ve built trusted relationships together.

The Resilience Alliance

Established in November 2023. Its roots lie in a run of compounding disasters: the 2017 floods, the 2019 bushfires, and the catastrophic floods and landslides of 2022. In each, local community organisers played a vital role in response, recovery and adaptation. Research in the years that followed found that these grassroots groups wanted stronger connections with each other for peer-to-peer support. That showed the need for regional collaboration in disaster resilience.

We support a network of place-based resilience groups to develop their own local approaches to risk reduction, preparedness and recovery, for fire, flood, landslides and whatever comes next.

The Resilience Alliance helps government agencies, councils and emergency services deliver on the commitments of the NSW State Emergency Management Plan, State Disaster Mitigation Plan and regional resilience priorities by connecting formal systems with trusted, grassroots community-led action.

Vision

Connected, collaborative Northern Rivers communities that are empowered to care for themselves and each other before, during and after disasters.

Purpose

To develop a self-sustaining, trusted Resilience Alliance of place-based, grassroots community-led resilience groups and organisations. The Resilience Alliance shares knowledge and resources, provides mutual support and wellbeing, takes collective action and advocates for identified community needs.

How we work

Through regular gatherings, deep conversations and shared experiences, the Resilience Alliance is building a grassroots community-led network focused on collective care, preparedness and regional strength.

  • Connect.

    60+ grassroots community-led groups are connected together across the region so they can build trusting relationships with each other so they know who to contact when the next climate disaster hits.

  • Share.

    Sharing knowledge, experience, practical tools and resources across the network so everyone can benefit from what others have learned.

  • Care.

    Peer to peer support, reducing the load, removing duplication and placing a real value on the efforts of all.

Since the Resilience Alliance formed in 2024, embedded researchers from the University Centre for Rural Health have worked alongside us to track how a new kind of regional collaboration takes shape. Resourcing Community Resilience -
The Help Fund

Grassroots community-led decisions about where funding goes

Groups told us that access to resources was a major barrier to keeping going, and that competitive grants can work against the collaboration resilience depends on. With support from the NRMA Help Fund, and in partnership with the Northern Rivers Community Foundation and Monash University’s Fire to Flourish, Resilience Alliance groups co-designed a participatory grantmaking approach. Establishment Grants of $3,000 and Resilience Action Grants of up to $10,000 are now funding radio networks, community hubs, disaster plans, preparedness training and wellbeing events.

Disaster Ready Three -
Connected and Prepared

Enabling regional readiness across the Northern Rivers

Connected and Prepared is helping the Resilience Alliance scale a proven, place-based model of grassroots community-led resilience. Over the life of the project (2026–2028), at least 60 grassroots resilience groups will receive tailored governance support, shared digital tools, capacity building and peer learning. That support is delivered by a regional team of six that reaches every LGA in the Northern Rivers.

Supported by the Australian Government through the Disaster Ready Fund.

The Steering Committee:

Is the coordinating body for the Resilience Alliance. It is made up of representatives from resilience-related groups and organisations across the region, and operates by agreement among groups and organisations rather than by reporting to a separate incorporated parent body. The steering committee actively aims to maintain a network that reflects the geographic and community diversity of the region.

Progress

Our progress so far

Our story started in the 2022 floods, when local people led the response in their own communities. Since then, those groups have come together as the Northern Rivers Community Resilience Alliance. Today we are 60+ groups and growing. Between June and October 2026 we have spoken with 58 of those groups to take stock: where we are now, and where we’d like to be.

Many groups, shared purpose

We are a diverse alliance of grassroots community-led groups and organisations: skilled by experience, varied by structure, and united in helping our communities care for themselves and each other before, during and after disasters.

We are disaster tested

Many of our groups have experienced disaster as a team, and local knowledge runs deep.

We value social connection

Connection is at the heart of what we do. Our groups build relationships in their own teams, across their community, with other groups in the alliance, and with emergency services and government agencies, so when disaster hits, people already know who to call.

We want to be ready for the next disaster

Taking stock also showed us where we want to grow. Seven aspirations stood out. Each one is something our groups told us they want support with, so we can look after our communities and each other for the long haul.

  1. 01 Create or update an action plan

    Only 13/58 groups say they have an up to date action plan for resilience.

  2. 02 Reduce key-people dependency

    38 groups noted that leadership/succession planning is a key priority for them.

  3. 03 Maintain engagement between disasters

    Community engagement, morale and the drive to stay prepared fade outside of disasters. Groups see this as a big gap.

  4. 04 Improve our knowledge of vulnerable residents

    About half of our groups told us they need help identifying vulnerable people in their community.

  5. 05 Grow our capacity to meet demands

    Groups lack time, people and steady money leading to volunteer burn out and good projects stalling before they finish.

  6. 06 Improve our backup communication systems

    At least 10 groups have specifically called this out as a gap. Without a backup, a community may be unable to communicate and coordinate during a disaster.

  7. 07 Manage our volunteer surges

    30 groups do not have volunteer policy, procedures, risk documentation or templates.

Privacy Policy

How NRCRA collects, uses and protects your personal information.

Draft, last updated 4 November 2026

  1. Draft

    1. Who we are

    The Northern Rivers Community Resilience Alliance (NRCRA) is a grassroots community-led network of local resilience groups across the Northern Rivers of New South Wales. In this policy, “we” and “us” mean NRCRA.

  2. Draft

    2. About this policy

    This policy explains how we collect, use, share and protect your personal information. We follow the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles. By using this website or giving us your information, you agree to this policy.

  3. Draft

    3. The information we collect

    Your name, email address, phone number, group and role. Anything you send us, such as emails, form entries, event registrations, feedback and answers in Health Checks. Resilience Alliance Community Knowledge (RACK) sign-in details, such as your name, email, group, role and sign-in times. Photos, videos and stories, with your permission. Basic technical details about your visit, such as your device, browser, IP address and the pages you view.

  4. Draft

    4. How we collect it

    Directly from you, through forms, emails, events, calls and RACK. From your group, when it nominates you for a role or an event. Automatically, through cookies and website analytics.

  5. Draft

    5. How we use it

    To run the network, events and training. To answer your questions and send updates you ask for. To manage RACK access. To learn and improve together, including research with the University Centre for Rural Health. To report to our funders. To keep this website working and secure. To meet our legal duties.

  6. Draft

    6. Stories, photos and quotes

    We only share your photo, video, story or quote with your permission. We can show a quote without your name if you prefer. You can change your mind at any time by emailing us, and we will remove it going forward.

  7. Draft

    7. Who we share it with

    We do not sell your personal information. We share it only with people and services that help us run the Resilience Alliance: our website, email, events and RACK providers, and our research partners, who must keep it confidential. We give funders summary or de-identified information. We share it with others only when you agree, or when the law requires it.

  8. Draft

    8. Other services on this website

    This website uses services from other companies. Wix hosts the site and provides website analytics. Google provides the map on our Network page. Vimeo plays our videos. Flourish shows our charts. We also link to Facebook and LinkedIn. These services may collect information and set cookies under their own privacy policies. Some of them store information outside Australia.

  9. Draft

    9. Cookies and analytics

    Cookies are small files saved on your device. We use them to make the website work and to see how people use it, so we can improve it. You can refuse or delete cookies in your browser settings, but some parts of the website may not work as well.

  10. Draft

    10. RACK sign-in

    RACK is our shared library of tools and resources for member groups. When you sign in, we collect your name, email, group, role and sign-in times to manage access and keep RACK secure. This policy applies to your use of RACK.

  11. Draft

    11. Storage and security

    We keep your information in secure systems and limit who can see it. We keep it only as long as we need it, then delete it or remove your details. No online system is completely secure. If a data breach is likely to cause you serious harm, we will tell you and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

  12. Draft

    12. Children and young people

    This website is for adults. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18 without a parent or guardian’s permission. If you think a young person has given us their details, email us and we will delete them.

  13. Draft

    13. Links to other websites

    Our website links to other websites, such as those of our partners and funders. We are not responsible for their privacy practices, so please read their policies.

  14. Draft

    14. Access and correction

    You can ask to see the personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. You can also unsubscribe from our updates at any time. We will reply within 30 days.

  15. Draft

    15. Complaints

    If you think we have handled your information badly, please tell us and we will look into it. If you are not happy with our response, you can contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner at oaic.gov.au or on 1300 363 992.

  16. Draft

    16. Changes to this policy

    We may update this policy from time to time. When we do, we will change the date at the top of this page.
