Brand colour contrast

Contrast ratios for the brand orange (#F05A28), yellow (#FFCB2A) and teal (#00A79D) against white, navy and black text. Ratios use the WCAG (opens in a new tab) formula, the same one the WebAIM Contrast Checker uses, and each row links to WebAIM with the colours filled in.

Orange & teal elements. To maintain readability (opens in a new tab), orange and teal shouldn’t be used as background colours for large chunks of text, as standard text colours (white, navy or black) won’t pass contrast requirements (opens in a new tab). They work well for smaller accents, like title backgrounds. For teal backgrounds, see the recommended colour adjustments.

Recommended colour adjustments for using teal as backgrounds

The brand teal #00A79D gives white text only 2.99:1, which fails AA (opens in a new tab). So the prototypes use an adjusted teal, #00857C, wherever white text sits on teal (every button, including Sign In). It is a little darker, and white on it reaches 4.52:1. Brand teal is kept for borders, dots and text on navy, where it passes. The adjusted teal is a new colour and needs the client’s yes.

Does teal work with orange?

Not side by side. Orange #F05A28 and teal #00A79D are opposite hues, but they are almost the same lightness, so the contrast between them is only 1.13:1. Anything that needs 3:1 against its neighbour (text, icons, chart segments, button edges) fails when one sits on the other.

Seen by different eyes

Simulated with standard colour-matrix filters. In greyscale the two squares become nearly the same grey, so orange and teal depend on hue alone to tell them apart.

  • Never: orange text on teal, teal text on orange, or a orange shape touching a teal shape with no gap that carries meaning (for example a two-colour chart or status).
  • Fine: orange and teal as separate blocks with their own navy text, as in the Connect, Share, Care band, where each colour has yellow or a gap between it and the next. Navy text works on both.
  • If they must touch: separate them with a navy or white line, at least 3px wide.

What this means

  • Yellow: white text fails everywhere (1.52:1). Navy passes comfortably (9.95:1), so yellow is safe for buttons and title backgrounds with navy text.
  • Orange: white fails for body text (3.39:1) and only passes as large text. Navy is 4.46:1, just under 4.5:1, so it only passes when the text is large (24px, or bold from about 19px). Black does pass (6.20:1) but is off brand.
  • Teal: white text is 2.99:1, which fails even as large text (it needs 3:1). Navy is 5.05:1, which passes the ratio, but it is not recommended: navy and this teal are hard to tell apart for some people with colour blindness. Black passes (7.01:1) but is off brand.
  • As text colour: orange on white is 3.39:1, yellow on white is 1.52:1 and teal on white is 2.99:1, so none of them works for words on a white page.
  • Adjusted teal: because brand teal fails with white text, the prototypes use a slightly darker teal, #00857C (white text 4.52:1), for buttons and any area with white text. Brand teal stays for borders, dots and text on navy. See Recommended colour adjustments.
